Prop firm trading is a discipline. Not just in execution — in record-keeping.

When you're on an FTMO challenge, a The5ers evaluation, or managing a FundedNext funded account, your numbers are being watched. The firm tracks your daily loss, your maximum drawdown, your consistency. You should be tracking them too — in your own journal, independently, before any surprises appear on their dashboard.

Most traders in funded accounts don't keep a proper journal. They either skip it entirely or use a spreadsheet that can't keep up with their trade volume. That gap — between what actually happened on your account and what you think happened — is where evaluations fail.

## Why Prop Firm Traders Need a Trading Journal

The obvious answer is rule compliance. Prop firms give you specific constraints: a daily loss limit, a total drawdown cap, a minimum trading day requirement. Breaching any of these ends your account.

A journal that captures your trades automatically gives you real-time awareness of where you stand against each limit — without having to pull up your broker history and do the maths while you're in a trade.

The less obvious answer is pattern recognition. Prop firm evaluations are stressful environments. Stress changes behaviour. Traders who perform well in their own practice accounts sometimes take larger position sizes, revenge trade, or override their own rules during evaluations. A complete, honest record makes that pattern visible — so you can address it before it costs you another evaluation fee.

## Tracking Daily Loss Limits and Drawdown

Prop firms measure drawdown differently. Some use balance-based drawdown; others use equity-based. Some calculate your daily loss limit from your starting daily balance; others from your peak balance. Getting this wrong can cost you an account.

BigMarkt's journal captures the raw trade data — your P&L per trade, running account balance, and trade-by-trade history. You can see exactly how much you've made or lost in a session at a glance.

Combined with your own understanding of the firm's specific rules, this gives you a ground-truth record that complements — rather than replaces — your own tracking.
