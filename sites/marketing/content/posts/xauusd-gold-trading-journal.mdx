## Why Gold Traders Need a Dedicated Journal

XAUUSD trading has characteristics that make accurate journaling particularly important — and particularly difficult to do manually.

**Speed and volatility.** Gold can move 150 pips in the 15 minutes around CPI. If you're managing an active trade and simultaneously trying to log it, one of those things suffers. Usually it's the log.

**Session dependency.** Gold has strong session biases. London open, New York overlap, and Asian range expansion all behave differently. Understanding which sessions you actually perform in — not which ones you think you do — requires a reliable dataset. That dataset only exists if you've captured every trade.

**Setup repetition.** Gold traders who use ICT concepts, SMC, or order flow approaches are often running the same setup across many trades. Evaluating whether your FVG entry on the London open actually has positive expectancy over 100 trades is only possible if you have 100 complete, accurate records.

## The Problem with Manually Logging Gold Trades

The obvious one: you forget. You're in a trade, managing it, watching price approach your TP — and when it closes, you move on. The journal entry waits. By the next session, the specifics have blurred.

The less obvious one: selective logging. When you log trades manually, you choose when to log. Trades that hit SL at 2am tend not to make the journal. Trades that ran to TP after a tense hold get detailed writeups. Over time, your manually logged win rate floats upward while your actual performance stays the same.

This isn't dishonesty. It's how memory works. The fix is to not rely on memory at all.
